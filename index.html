Mock reviews

The signature service

Know your reviews before they’re written.

A mock review is a private review of your unreleased game, written the way a critic would write it at launch, score and all. You read it while there is still time to change things.

The practice is led by Brian Crecente and backed by a vetted bench of more than 50 working and former games critics in eight countries. Every engagement runs under NDA and client lists stay private. A standing recusal policy keeps this work separate from published journalism.

Five formats

Service and rate card on request.

  • Signature Mock Review

    A full pre-release review written by Brian Crecente, played to completion and scored. Includes a predicted Metacritic band, a Steam sentiment outlook, prioritized fixes, comparables and a live debrief with your team.

    Written by Crecente

  • Critical Panel

    Three to five critics from the bench review your game independently, each chosen for genre, region and generation. Crecente writes the synthesis: where they agree, where they split, and what to fix.

    3–5 critics

  • Game Evaluation

    For games still in development, from vertical slice through beta. An analytical report on vision, systems, genre positioning and audience. No score predictions before beta.

    In development

  • Readiness Review

    A fast, focused read on one beat before the public sees it: a Steam Next Fest demo, a store page, a trailer or an announcement package. Written by a senior critic from the bench, edited by Crecente.

    One week

  • Staged Evaluation Program

    Checkpoints from vertical slice through gold, closing with a full mock review or panel near gold, so launch reviews confirm what you already know.

    Slice to gold

The founder

Brian Crecente

Founder, Pad & Pixel, LLC

Brian founded Kotaku, co-founded Polygon, and was video games editor at Rolling Stone and at Variety. Before he covered games full time, he spent more than 15 years as an award-winning reporter at major daily newspapers around the country, covering wildfires, daily crime, a contested presidential election and serial killers. He has written about video games since 2000.
