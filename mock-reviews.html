What it is

Who it’s for and what you get

Set out like a published review. Kept private.

Who it’s for
Studios and publishers with a game on the way.
When to start
As early as a vertical slice or as late as near-gold. It can also cover a single public moment before it happens: a Steam Next Fest demo, a store page, a trailer or an announcement package.
What you get
A written review or report. Depending on the format, it comes with a predicted Metacritic band, a Steam sentiment outlook, prioritized fixes and a live debrief with your team.

Who writes them

Brian Crecente and the bench

Written by people who review games.

The practice is led by Brian Crecente, founder of Kotaku and co‑founder of Polygon. He is backed by a vetted bench of more than 50 working and former games critics in eight countries.

Brian Crecente
Writes every Signature Mock Review himself. Writes the synthesis for every Critical Panel and edits every Readiness Review.
The bench

The panel

Choosing a format

Service and rate card on request

Five formats, depending on where the game is.

From a one-week read of a single demo or trailer to checkpoints from vertical slice through gold.

01

Signature Mock Review

Written by Brian Crecente himself.

A full pre-release review. Brian plays the game to completion, scores it and argues the score.

In the review
A predicted Metacritic band and Steam sentiment outlook, strengths and weaknesses, prioritized fixes and an analysis of comparable games.
Afterward
A live debrief with your team.
Fee
Fixed, by game tier. No hourly billing.
02

Critical Panel

Three to five critics, one synthesis.

Critics from the bench write mock reviews of your game independently. Brian writes the synthesis on top.

The critics
Each picked for genre expertise, region and generation.
The synthesis
Where the critics converge, where they split, and what to fix, lead with and brace for.
03

How an engagement runs

Typical turnaround is two to three weeks. A Readiness Review takes one.

  1. NDA
  2. Build access
  3. Played and written
  4. Report and live debrief

Confidentiality

Every engagement runs under NDA, and client lists stay private. A standing recusal policy keeps this work separate from published journalism.
